## Logical ownership and visual softness

Treat the four equal slices as exact logical abstraction zones, not four hard-clipped panels. Use them to determine Reality Anchor ownership and the dominant Reality, 30%, 65%, and 90% treatments. Keep every slice at exactly 25% of the slicing dimension, but do not reveal the three boundaries as straight lines, gutters, abrupt masks, full-height or full-width style switches, or collage borders unless explicitly requested.

Make the viewer perceive one visual world passing through four artistic states. Keep those states identifiable through the dominant treatment in each slice core, not through visible dividers.

## Transition zones

Create a narrow visual transition zone centered on each logical boundary, typically about 3%–8% of the full image dimension along the slicing direction. Treat this as a guideline rather than a pixel rule.

Allow rendering influence to overlap slightly between abstract slices without resizing slices, relocating the anchor, changing level ownership, or inventing a fifth abstraction level. At an Anchor boundary, keep generated influence on the abstract side unless a deterministic, non-generative operation is used outside every protected face. Keep the center or body of each slice strongly representative of its assigned state. Do not turn the full composition into a smooth `30 → 35 → ... → 65` gradient.

Permit even a Reality-to-90% adjacency. Achieve coherence with shared structure and visual language; never reorder levels merely to create an easier numeric transition.

## Continuity methods

Prefer semantic and artistic continuity to blur or generic feathering. Use one or more of these methods:

1. **Contour continuation:** Carry body silhouettes, shoulder lines, horizons, rooflines, building edges, roads, or landscape contours across the boundary while changing their rendering language from photographic edge to sketch, simplified line, painterly edge, or source-derived geometry.
2. **Shape continuation:** Continue a major object across the boundary as a simplified color mass, contour, painted region, or plane instead of physically cutting it at the seam.
3. **Color continuity:** Echo dominant and accent colors, related tonal values, or a progressively simplified palette near adjacent edges. Avoid instant unrelated palette changes unless extreme contrast is deliberately required.
4. **Texture migration:** Introduce sparse pencil strokes, brush marks, or other source-derived texture before the boundary and let it become dominant after the boundary.
5. **Controlled intrusion:** Let sketch lines, paint strokes, color blocks, geometric planes, contours, or environmental shapes extend slightly between abstract zones. Do not intrude into protected Anchor pixels or a primary face. Use intrusion to hide mechanical seams without erasing four-state ownership.
6. **Organic transition path:** Let the perceived change follow a subject contour, clothing edge, building outline, shadow, brush stroke, color mass, or environmental shape even though the mathematical boundary remains straight.

Use subtle tonal blending only when structural methods are insufficient. Use blur only as a minor finishing tool, never the primary solution.

## Reality Anchor and subject protection

Treat the entire Reality Anchor as a source-preserved protected region. Do not generate, repaint, or blend over it. Build transitions on the abstract side of its boundaries. Permit only deterministic non-structural grading outside protected facial pixels; when that is unavailable, leave the entire Anchor unchanged.

If the primary face is near a boundary, protect it over seamlessness. Do not split its rendering style, geometrize one side, fragment features, or mismatch photographic and illustrated facial halves. Keep the source face pixels intact. Route the transition entirely through generated background or non-face content when possible; otherwise accept a visible boundary.

For human figures crossing a boundary, preserve body silhouette, pose, gesture, and head/body relationship. Transition deliberately from photographic body to simplified body, contour, color block, or painterly mass. Avoid misaligned versions, repeated limbs, double faces, ghost edges, or offset cutouts.

For architecture crossing a boundary, preserve silhouette, roofline, perspective direction, and primary massing. Transform the same building from photographic façade to simplified façade, architectural sketch, planes, or color masses; never hard-cut to an unrelated geometric building.

For landscape, preserve horizon and dominant directional flow. For sky or flat low-information backgrounds, allow greater freedom in color and texture migration.

## Boundary resolution priority

Resolve a visible seam in this order:

1. Continue important silhouettes.
2. Continue semantic structures.
3. Echo neighboring colors.
4. Overlap line, paint, texture, or shape.
5. Follow an organic perceived boundary.
6. Add subtle tonal blending if needed.
7. Apply minimal blur only as a last finishing tool.

Prefer deliberate simplification over duplicated photographic fragments when exact reconstruction is difficult. Never use the same generic feathered mask at every boundary.

## Boundary validation

Inspect all three boundaries before delivery. Refine any boundary that has:

- an obvious straight divider, gutter, or rectangular mask;
- a mechanically cut scene or instantaneous full-edge style change;
- an interrupted important silhouette, horizon, roofline, or semantic structure;
- a severed building or misaligned human body;
- a double outline, ghost edge, repeated limb, offset object copy, white halo, or sticker-like edge;
- a transition that damages a primary face or the Reality Anchor's photographic identity;
- so much blending that the four dominant states are no longer distinguishable.

Accept a boundary only when it feels intentional, organic, semantically continuous, and visually integrated. The abstraction states should reveal the four-part logic; the seams should not announce it.

Exception: accept a noticeable seam when further blending would modify the primary face or overwrite the source-preserved Anchor. Protection outranks seamlessness.
